Your Response Protocol

A Practicum to Shape Your Own Church's Path Forward

Learning objectives: By the end of this module, you will have produced a one-page response protocol, in your own words, that you can use in your own ministry context, and you will be able to explain your reasoning for each part of it.

1. Why This Module Is a Practicum, Not a Lecture

The first three modules gave you theological grounding, pastoral posture, and a framework for ministering to experiencers. This module does not add new content. It asks you to synthesize everything into something concrete you can actually use: a one-page protocol you could hand to an associate pastor, refer to before a difficult conversation, or simply keep in your own desk drawer as a touchstone.

This is the module that turns ten hours of training into something durable in your ministry.

2. What a Response Protocol Is For

A response protocol is not a script. It is not meant to be read aloud to a congregant. It exists so that in a moment when someone brings this to you unexpectedly, tired, on a Sunday morning, in a hospital waiting room, you are not building your response from scratch. You already know where you stand and how you want to show up.

The best protocols are short enough to actually be used. One page. Five sections. Written in language you would actually use, not academic language borrowed from this course.

3. Building Your Protocol, Section by Section

Work through each section below. Pull directly from the reflections you wrote at the end of Modules I through III, you have already done most of this thinking.

Section 1: My Theological Position (2-3 sentences). Pull from your Module I reflection. State, in plain language, where you stand on UAP/NHI as part of creation, and your working position on discernment versus default suspicion around deception.

Section 2: My Opening Posture (2-3 sentences). Pull from your Module II reflection. What will you say and do in the first minute of a conversation, whether it's a speculative question or a personal disclosure? What is your go-to reassurance phrase that doesn't minimize?

Section 3: If Someone Discloses a Personal Experience (3-4 sentences). Pull from your Module III reflection. What will you say in the first thirty seconds? What are your specific referral triggers, the situations that tell you to bring in additional support alongside your own pastoral care?

Section 4: Protecting Confidentiality and Managing Congregational Tension (2-3 sentences). How will you handle information about an experiencer's story if it starts to circulate? What is your plan for addressing the topic generally, from the pulpit or in teaching, without identifying anyone?

Section 5: My Own Ongoing Posture (1-2 sentences). Name how you intend to keep learning and stay open on this topic rather than treating this course as the final word. What is one next step, a book, a conversation, staying current on the research, that keeps this posture alive past today?

4. A Worked Example

Below is a filled-in example, written in a composite pastoral voice, not attributed to any specific study participant, to show you the tone and length you're aiming for. Yours should sound like you, not like this.

My Theological Position: I believe creation is wider than what we can currently explain, and I don't think the existence of unexplained phenomena threatens the truth of who God is. At the same time, I take seriously that not everything unexplained is safe, and I hold both curiosity and caution without letting either one collapse into the other.

My Opening Posture: I will not rush to reassure before I've actually heard what's underneath the question. My first move is to ask what part of this is unsettling them, not to correct or redirect.

If Someone Discloses a Personal Experience: I will thank them for trusting me and ask them to tell me more before I say anything interpretive. I will listen carefully and let them set the pace before we talk together about what it might mean spiritually. I will consider a counseling referral, alongside my own continued support, if I see ongoing sleep disruption, functional impairment, or isolation from our community.

Protecting Confidentiality: I will treat this the way I treat any pastoral disclosure, as confidential by default. If the topic needs addressing congregationally, I will do it in general terms from the pulpit rather than referencing anyone specifically.

My Own Ongoing Posture: I intend to keep reading and stay in conversation with other pastors navigating this, rather than treating this course as a finished answer.
